## Tool gate

**Final status: No tool (controller ruling).** There is no shelter-time tool in v7.4; Tasks 2 and 3 are not run.
surv-fallout-how-long keeps the 7:10 rule as text, exactly as sourced, with the dated 1985 table.

Facts behind it (writer's finding, confirmed by the batch A reviewer on re-opening):

- The only current federal statement of the rule found is FEMA Center for Domestic Preparedness (CDP) online course
  AWR-923-W "Radiological Emergency Management", page "The 7:10 Rule of Thumb":
  https://cdp.dhs.gov/shared/se/courses/default/AWR-923-W%2005122021%201.2-20210512144644/groups/394.html
  Wording: "The 7:10 Rule of Thumb states that for every 7-fold increase in time after detonation, there is a 10-fold
  decrease in the exposure rate." It is introduced as a way to predict future exposure rates "From the exposure rate
  determined by a survey instrument". One example: "let's say that 2 hours after detonation the exposure rate is 400 R/hr.
  After 14 hours, the exposure rate will be 1/10 as much, or 40 R/hr." An image repeats the rule. No table of rows (the
  page's HTML tables are layout only).
- "Limitations of 7:10 Rule of Thumb" (groups/395.html): answers are only approximations; assumes the detonation time is
  known and fallout from only one detonation; "nothing can replace a direct instrument reading"; the rule may still be
  needed to estimate when short trips outside a shelter are possible.
- Neither page's text names FEMA, the CDP or DHS; only the URL does. FEMA is identified by the catalogue
  (https://cdp.dhs.gov/online_course: "REM AWR-923-W • Version: 4", logo alt text "Department of Homeland Security Shield /
  FEMA", header link "FEMA Center for Domestic Preparedness"). The pages opened sit in a folder labelled "1.2", dated
  2021-05-12, so they may not be the current course version.
- Rows relative to 1 hour (7 hours 1/10, 49 hours 1/100, about 2 weeks 1/1,000) come from FEMA's 1985 Protection in the
  Nuclear Age (dated) or are derived from the rule; no current source prints them.
- Looked for, not found (current sources with the rule as text or table): FEMA, Planning Guidance for Response to a Nuclear
  Detonation, third edition (2022), all of it, and its December 2024 fact sheet (reviewer); FEMA, Nuclear Detonation
  Response Guidance: Planning for the First 72 Hours (2023); CDC public and clinician pages; Ready.gov Radiation
  Emergencies; HHS REMM (only archive copies returned); EPA (only snapshot archives, including the superseded 2010 second
  edition); DHS S&T "Visualizing a Nuclear Detonation" fact sheet. The LLNL report "Key Response Planning Factors for the
  Aftermath of Nuclear Terrorism" is not a FEMA publication and was not opened.

## Meter

No official source opened gives meter readings a member of the public can act on. Looked in: Ready.gov Radiation
Emergencies; CDC Get Inside, Stay Inside, What to Do, Where To Go, Preparing for a Radiation Emergency (no mention of
detectors at all); FEMA planning guidance, third edition (detectors for responders, and planner zone thresholds only);
FEMA 72-hour guidance ("Only emergency workers with radiation detection equipment should operate outdoors"); the CDP
7:10 pages ("nothing can replace a direct instrument reading"). The dated 1968 and 1985 handbooks say only that without
instruments you depend on local government to say when to leave. So surv-fallout-how-long's "If you have a meter" section
says a meter is the only way to know the level and that the app does not yet explain how to read one, and meter guidance
is deferred (below). There is no tool and no meter input anywhere (spec 6, call 4).
