1. **No bleach soak for baby feeding items.** CDC's infant-items soak (1 teaspoon per 1/2 gallon, 2 minutes, not rinsed) would be a
   third household mix against the two-mix decision; only CDC's boiling method (5 minutes) is given, with a labelled caution not to
   use Clean and disinfect's mixes in its place (D141). Cost if wrong: a household with no fuel cannot sanitize a newborn's bottles
   from the app.
2. **Drying stops short.** The complete beginner methods opened (Penn State Extension, Let's Preserve; NCHFP) need an oven or
   dehydrator held at a set temperature (135 to 150°F) and, for outdoor-dried fruit, a freezer or oven to pasteurize; outdoors only
   fruit, at 85°F or more and below 60 percent humidity for several days, which Penn State calls impractical in Pennsylvania's
   humidity. A household without a freezer is assumed to be without power, so no method is given; the section gives the sources'
   conditions and names where to learn in normal times (D147). Cost if wrong: households with a generator, or in a dry spell, get no
   drying method.
3. **Salting, curing and smoking stop short.** NCHFP's curing and smoking document is a literature review with critical points, not a
   beginner method; it cures meat in a refrigerator (35 to 40°F), warns of a lethal nitrite mistake, and says smoke alone does not
   preserve. Each section says so in two or three sentences and stops (spec 10 call 4). Cost if wrong: none to safety.
4. **Cold storage is links only.** Keeping food cold is the hub's; root cellars and garden storage are deferred (spec 10 call 5).

7. **Dish towels.** FDA allows a clean dish towel for baby items; CDC's emergency page says never; CDC used (more cautious).
8. **Breast milk.** CDC's two pages read together: still has ice crystals, can be refrozen; completely thawed, never refrozen. The
