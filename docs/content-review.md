## Open questions for Jordan

- **Improvised tourniquet width (bleeding).** AHA-FA (2024, 9.1, Class 2a C-EO) says at least 2 inches wide; ARC-DB (Improvised tourniquets, 2025 update) says at least 1.5 inches. Used AHA (higher authority): 2 inches.
- **Open chest wound cover (chest-wound).** AHA-FA 2024 (9.2) and ARC-DB: reasonable to leave the wound open to air or cover with a clean non-sealing dry dressing; vented chest seal only by trained responders; routine chest seal by untrained lay responders not recommended. TCCC 2026: every open/sucking chest wound gets a vented chest seal immediately, and an unvented seal if no vented one is available. Used AHA/Red Cross for the main path; the protocol offers a vented seal only to trained users with one, explains the TCCC position in the detail, and does not tell lay users to use an unvented seal. If your group carries vented seals and is TCCC-trained, you may want the seal path to be the default.
- **Infant chest thrust technique (choking-infant).** AHA-PBLS 2025 uses the heel of one hand for infant chest thrusts (the two-finger technique was dropped for CPR compressions and replaced for thrusts). The Red Cross lay infant choking page (read 2026-11-16) still shows two fingers in the centre of the chest just below the nipple line. Used AHA: heel of one hand. Red Cross gives the depth (about 1.5 inches) and the positioning, which AHA does not contradict; kept those.
- **Choking sequence edition (choking, choking-infant).** Recorded for clarity: AHA 2025 (AHA-ABLS, AHA-PBLS, AHA-HL) starts adults and children with 5 back blows alternating with 5 abdominal thrusts; earlier AHA editions started with abdominal thrusts. Red Cross lay page also uses 5 back blows and 5 abdominal thrusts. No disagreement now; noted because users trained before 2025 on AHA may expect abdominal thrusts first.
- **Second epinephrine dose timing (anaphylaxis).** ARC-LAY: second dose if signs persist 5 to 10 minutes after the first and EMS has not arrived. ARC-DB: second dose may be considered for continued signs within 5 to 15 minutes. AHA-FA: repeat dose may be considered if no response and EMS will be more than 5 to 10 minutes. Used 5 to 10 minutes (AHA and Red Cross lay agree); the timer is set to 5 minutes as the earliest point. Off-grid, EMS will always be more than 10 minutes away, so in practice the second dose is given at 5 to 10 minutes if signs continue.
- **Discoloured auto-injector (anaphylaxis).** ARC-DB says a cloudy or discoloured solution should be discarded. It does not say what to do when it is the only dose available in the field. The protocol states the Red Cross position only. You may want medical advice on this for your group.
- **Spine injury with a compromised airway (recovery-position).** Sources say leave a possible spine injury as found unless moving is needed for safety, CPR or bleeding control. They do not address an unresponsive trauma casualty who is breathing but vomiting. The protocol does not invent an answer; it says to roll for CPR if breathing stops. Consider asking a medical adviser.
- **Lone rescuer with no signal (CPR child/infant) — updated after review.** The AHA 2025 Pediatric BLS single-rescuer algorithm (accessible long-description PDF, https://cpr.heart.org/-/media/CPR-Files/CPR-Guidelines-Files/2025-Accessible/Algorithm-BLS-Ped-Single-Rescuer-LngDscrp-250618-Ed.pdf, Boxes 5, 5a and 7) covers infants and children: if the sudden collapse was witnessed, activate the emergency response and get the AED first; otherwise give CPR, and after 2 minutes, if still alone, activate the emergency response and get the AED. ARC-LAY says the same for babies. Both cpr-child and cpr-infant now state this, attributed to AHA. Off-grid "activate" can mean leaving for hours; the protocols state the AHA rule and the trade-off and leave the decision to the rescuer. Which should the group follow?
- **Adult lone rescuer with no signal (cpr-adult).** AHA-ABLS 2025 (Class 1, B-NR): a lone responder should activate the emergency response system first, then immediately begin CPR; supporting text adds that local circumstances decide whether to delay CPR to activate EMS. The first version told users to start CPR straight away. It now states AHA's advice and the off-grid trade-off (help may be hours away) and leaves the choice to the user. Which should the group follow off-grid?
- **Second-dose frequency (anaphylaxis).** AHA-FA 2024 6.5 supportive text: 7% to 18% of people with anaphylaxis need more than one dose. ARC-DB: 18 to 35 percent. Used AHA (higher authority).
- **Possible spine injury found face down (recovery-position).** ARC-LAY (unresponsive and breathing): with a suspected head, neck or back injury, leave in the position found unless moving for safety, CPR or bleeding. ILCOR 2026 CoSTR FA 7040 (good-practice statement) and ARC-DB Positioning: people found face down, prone, or in neck or torso flexion should be repositioned supine for reassessment. The protocol states both in s1's detail; the spine branch keeps "leave as found". Which should apply?
- **Tourniquet time on long evacuations (bleeding).** TCCC 2026: reassess every tourniquet; convert to a hemostatic or pressure dressing within 2 hours where the casualty is not in shock, the wound can be watched, and it is not an amputation; ASM/CLS personnel should not convert beyond 2 hours unless directed; do not remove after 6 hours without monitoring and lab capability. Off-grid evacuations will often exceed 2 hours and the protocol says only "leave it on". Should the group be trained in conversion (Advanced procedures)?
- **Abdominal and other torso wounds (bleeding).** The bleeding protocol now sends belly and other body wounds to firm direct pressure only (AHA-FA 9.1 Class 1 direct pressure). ARC-DB's bleeding algorithm lists "wound packing, if trained" for head, neck and trunk wounds; STB v2 lists packing locations without excluding the body. Should the group pack a heavily bleeding belly wound, or press only?
- **Adult chest-thrust hand position (choking) — round 2.** No AHA or Red Cross page that could be opened gives a hand position (AHA-ABLS cpr.heart.org page and the AHA adult FBAO algorithm give indications only; ARC-LAY gives indications only; the AHA full text on ahajournals could not be reached). The technique in choking s8 now comes from MedlinePlus (US National Library of Medicine, https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/000047.htm, review date 2026-02-17), which is outside the AHA/Red Cross tier: arms around the chest, fist on the middle of the breastbone between the nipples, firm backward thrusts. Mayo Clinic (https://www.mayoclinic.org/first-aid/first-aid-choking/basics/art-20056637, not on the authority list) gives a lower landmark: hands at the base of the breastbone, just above the joining of the lowest ribs. Which landmark should the group train?
- **Chest-thrust trigger in pregnancy (choking).** AHA-ABLS 2025: chest thrusts in the "late stages of pregnancy". ARC-LAY: anyone "obviously pregnant or known to be pregnant" (a broader trigger). The step states both. Which should the group use?
- **Spine injury with a compromised airway (recovery-position, spine-injury) — Task 11 addition.** MedlinePlus Spinal injury (https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/000029.htm, reviewed 2025-06-04, outside the AHA/Red Cross tier) answers part of the earlier question: roll a person with a possible spine injury only if they are vomiting or choking on blood, or to check breathing; use at least two people, one holding the head, and turn them as one unit keeping head, neck and back in line. AHA-FA 2024 9.4 (stay as still as possible unless safety warrants movement) and the Red Cross lay page (unless moving for CPR or bleeding control) do not mention vomiting. spine-injury s3/s4 now use the MedlinePlus technique, attributed. MedlinePlus also says CPR on a possible spine injury should be compressions only without moving the neck; AHA includes breaths and the cpr protocols allow a jaw thrust. The spine protocol states both. Is the MedlinePlus roll acceptable to you as the group's method?
- **Straightening a broken limb with poor circulation (fracture).** AHA-FA 2024 9.7 (Class 2b, C-EO): treat a deformed broken limb in the position found unless straightening is needed for safe and prompt transport; no evidence that first aid providers can reduce fractures safely. Both MedlinePlus pages (outside the tier) give the same exception. Broken bone: if circulation is poor and trained personnel are not quickly available, try to realign the limb into a normal resting position. How to make a splint: do not change the position of, or realign, an injured part, but if medical help is not available and the part looks abnormally bent and the limb is pale, gently placing it back in its normal position may improve circulation (both re-read 2026-11-17, round 2). fracture s8 states both, and its action text and warn exclude hip, pelvis and thigh injuries (MedlinePlus Broken bone: do not move these unless absolutely necessary). fracture s8 states AHA first, then the MedlinePlus off-grid option. Should the group be trained to realign a pulseless limb, or follow AHA strictly?
- **Cold on a sprain: 20 to 30 minutes or 20 on, 20 off (sprain-dislocation, fracture).** AHA-FA 2024 9.6: limit cold to 20 to 30 minutes per application (supportive text: 3 to 4 times a day). Red Cross lay pages (Fractures, Muscle Bone and Joint Injury, Wounds): no more than 20 minutes, then wait 20 minutes; 10 on/10 off if 20 minutes is not tolerated. The timers are set to 20 minutes (inside both); sprain-dislocation states both; fracture states the Red Cross rule (AHA 9.7 says nothing about cold on fractures).
- **Ice on burns (burns).** AHA-FA 2024 9.8 (Class 2b, C-LD): if clean running water is unavailable, superficial burns with intact skin may be cooled with ice wrapped in cloth, for no more than 10 minutes. Red Cross lay Burns page: do not use ice ("may further damage the skin"); use a cold, non-freezing compress. burns s5 gives the Red Cross compress first and the AHA ice option with its limits.
- **Petroleum jelly on a burn (burns).** AHA-FA 2024 9.8 (Class 2b, B-R): after cooling, small partial-thickness burns managed at home may be dressed with petrolatum, petrolatum-based antibiotic ointment, honey or aloe under a non-stick dressing. Red Cross lay Burns page: do not put butter, mayonnaise or petroleum jelly on a burn (traps heat). burns s8 says no grease on a fresh burn and gives the AHA option for later care of small burns, attributed.
- **Chemical in the eye: how long to flush (eye-injury, burns).** AHA-FA 2024 8.3 (Class 1, C-LD): 15 minutes of tap water. MedlinePlus Eye emergencies: at least 15 minutes. Red Cross lay Eye Injury and Burns pages: keep flushing until EMS arrives. Off-grid EMS may be hours away. The timer is 15 minutes; eye-injury s2 states all three.
- **Covering both eyes (eye-injury).** MedlinePlus Eye emergencies: loosely bandage both eyes for an embedded object, and cover both eyes if blood pools in the eye, to limit eye movement. TCCC CLS Module 05 (2020): do not cover both eyes unless both are injured (military reason: the casualty can keep functioning). AHA-FA 9.10 says only to tape a shield or cup over the eye. eye-injury s5 states both.
- **Tourniquet for every limb amputation (amputation).** TCCC 2026 TFC 3a: apply a limb tourniquet for any traumatic amputation. MedlinePlus Amputation - traumatic: do not use a tourniquet unless bleeding is life-threatening. MedlinePlus also says to raise the injured area, which AHA-FA 9.1 and ARC say does not help (bleeding protocol don'ts). amputation s2 uses TCCC (higher on the authority list) for arm or leg amputations and direct pressure for fingers and toes, and leaves out elevation.
- **Impaled object exceptions (impaled-object).** The task asked for the exceptions the source gives to "leave it in place". None of the sources that could be opened give any: Red Cross lay (Life-threatening external bleeding, embedded objects) and MedlinePlus (Cuts and puncture wounds) say only do not remove. EMS and military teaching often allows removal when the object blocks the airway or prevents CPR, but no source for that was opened, so the protocol gives no exception. Do you want one, and from which source?
- **Antibiotic ointment on wounds (wound-care).** AHA-FA 2024 9.3 supportive text: antibiotic or antibacterial dressings have not been shown to improve healing or reduce infection in clean wounds. WMS 2014 update (grade 2C): topical antibiotics may help, with little downside in people who are not allergic. MedlinePlus: apply antibacterial ointment. wound-care s7 states all three without choosing.
- **Draining blisters (blisters).** WMS 2014 update (grade 1C): drain blisters larger than 5 mm but leave the roof on. NHS Blisters (UK, not on the authority list): do not burst a blister yourself; a doctor may drain a large or painful one with a sterilised needle. blisters s3 follows WMS and states the NHS position. No source opened describes how a lay person should drain one; the step says sterilised needle (from the NHS description of what a doctor does) and leaves the roof in place.
- **Sleep after a concussion when no provider can check them (head-injury).** Red Cross Concussion: sleeping is generally considered safe once a health care provider has checked the person; the provider may recommend periodic waking. CDC: being very drowsy or impossible to wake is a danger sign. No source gives a waking schedule for someone who has not been checked. head-injury s8 lets them sleep with someone watching and treats difficulty waking as a danger sign. Do you want a waking interval, and from which medical adviser?
- **CPR in traumatic cardiac arrest (march).** AHA: CPR for anyone unresponsive and not breathing normally. TCCC 2026 TFC 17: battlefield CPR for blast or penetrating trauma with no pulse, no breathing and no signs of life will not succeed and should not be attempted; torso or polytrauma casualties first get bilateral needle decompression. march s16 sends the user to CPR and states the TCCC position. Off-grid, which should the group follow?
- **MARCH source level (march).** The newest All Service Members (ASM) curriculum document that could be opened is the 2019 ASM Rapid Casualty Assessment skill sheet; the TCCC 2026 guidelines no longer name the MARCH sequence or an ASM tier (they refer to ASM/CLS personnel). march follows the 2019 ASM sheet for the order and lay-level actions and TCCC 2026 for the clinical content. - **Cleaning major wounds (wound-care) — Task 11 review.** MedlinePlus Cuts and puncture wounds: "Do not try to clean a major wound, especially after the bleeding is under control." - **Vomiting as a danger sign after a head injury (head-injury).** Red Cross Concussion lists nausea or vomiting as needing immediate emergency care and says to call 911 for vomiting. CDC and AHA say repeated vomiting. Off-grid, does a single vomit trigger evacuation?

- **Blister draining trigger (blisters) — update.** WMS 2014: drain blisters over 5 mm, keep the roof. - **Breathing and pulse check in a very cold casualty (hypothermia, frostbite).** WMS hypothermia 2019 (Grade 1C): feel for a carotid pulse for 1 minute; if none, start CPR with rescue breathing. AHA 2025 Part 10 section 17: pulse and breathing may be very slow and hard to detect. The AHA lay-rescuer rule used in the CPR protocols is no pulse check and a quick look at breathing. hypothermia s1 and s15 tell the user to watch and feel for breathing for up to 1 minute (the WMS time, applied to breathing for a lay user) before deciding; the pulse check is in the detail. Is a 1-minute check acceptable for the group, or should lay users go straight to CPR?
- **Where to put heat packs (hypothermia).** WMS 2019 (1B): armpits, chest and back, in that order; never the limbs. TCCC 2026 (march s13): front of the chest and armpits. CDC Preventing Hypothermia: centre of the body, chest, neck, head and groin. AHA 2024: none on the limbs, no site named. hypothermia s6/s13 use WMS (consistent with TCCC) and mention CDC in the detail.
- **Frostbite rewarming water (frostbite).** AHA 2024 7.8 (Class 1) and the Red Cross lay steps: 99 to 104 F (37 to 40 C); studies used 20 to 30 minutes (AHA). The same Red Cross page's FAQ: not more than 100 to 105 F (38 to 40.5 C). MedlinePlus Frostbite: 104 to 108 F (40 to 42.2 C) for 15 to 30 minutes. WMS 2019 (AAFP 2020 summary and Medical Dialogues 2019 summary; the WMS 2024 full text could not be opened): 98.6 to 102.2 F (37 to 39 C), kept in range with a thermometer; without one, an uninjured extremity in the water for 30 seconds; rewarming typically complete within 30 minutes. Review round 1 (controller ruling): frostbite s9 now uses the overlap of AHA and WMS, 99 to 102 F (37 to 39 C), so neither is contradicted; the warning keeps AHA's harm figure (113 F (45 C) harmful in animal studies). Timer 30 minutes, stop when soft and colour returns. Which range should the group use?
- **Rewarming frostbite yourself (frostbite).** MedlinePlus How to prevent frostbite and hypothermia: never rewarm frostbite on your own. AHA 2024 and Red Cross: rewarm in lukewarm water at the earliest opportunity if it cannot refreeze. Used AHA; off-grid there is usually no one else to do it.
- **Frostnip (frostbite).** No AHA, Red Cross or CDC page that could be opened defines frostnip; the definition and care (red, cold skin that may go white but stays soft; warm against skin; do not rub) are from MedlinePlus (outside the top tier). - **Ibuprofen for frostbite (frostbite).** AHA 2024 7.8 (Class 2b, C-EO): ibuprofen may be reasonable to prevent tissue damage and treat pain; no dose and no exclusions given. frostbite s11 gives it "as the package directs" with the app's by-mouth exclusions in the warning. - **Heat stroke evacuation (heat-illness).** AHA 2024 7.9 (Class 1): activate emergency services for heat stroke. - **Pulse check after a lightning strike (lightning).** CDC Lightning first aid: check for breathing and a pulse (carotid or femoral). AHA lay rule (CPR protocols): no pulse check. lightning s5 asks only about responsiveness and breathing, and sends to the CPR protocols.
- **Lightning source edition (lightning).** The WMS lightning guideline 2014 update (Wilderness Environ Med 2014;25:S86-S95) full text could not be opened (wemjournal.org client error; the wmsdev mirror is blocked to automated fetching). - **Rescue breaths in the water (drowning).** MedlinePlus Non-fatal drowning: begin rescue breathing as soon as you can, even before reaching land. - **Abdominal thrusts after drowning (drowning).** WMS 2024: not recommended at any time. - **Vomiting during CPR after drowning (drowning).** No source that could be opened gives a technique. - **Drowning release after observation (drowning).** WMS 2024: release may be considered only if there are no symptoms other than a mild cough and the lungs sound normal through a stethoscope; ideally someone stays for 6 hours. - **Stopping CPR after cold-water drowning (drowning, hypothermia).** WMS drowning 2024: stopping might be reasonable after more than 30 minutes under water warmer than 6 C (43 F), more than 90 minutes in colder water, or after 25 minutes of continuous CPR. WMS hypothermia 2019: continue resuscitation regardless of core temperature; recoveries after 6.5 hours of CPR. drowning s3 gives the WMS drowning criteria and points to the Hypothermia protocol for a cold casualty. - **Carbon monoxide: snow shelters and the Poison Help line (carbon-monoxide).** CPSC names tents, campers and vehicles as enclosed spaces where fuel-burning heaters and lanterns must not run during sleep; carbon-monoxide s6 adds snow shelters as an enclosed space (no opened source names them). - **Field rewarming threshold (frostbite) — review round 1.** WMS 2019 (AAFP 2020 and Medical Dialogues 2019 summaries): rapid rewarming in the field only if definitive care is more than 2 hours away and refreezing can be prevented; otherwise let it thaw spontaneously, or keep it frozen until a thawed state can be kept. AHA 2024: rewarm at the earliest opportunity if there is no risk of refreezing. frostbite s8b detail states the WMS threshold; the protocol follows AHA. Is the 2-hour threshold relevant off-grid?
- **Walking out on thawed feet (frostbite) — review round 1.** WMS 2019 (Medical Dialogues summary): avoid walking on a thawed foot unless only the tips of the toes are affected. WMS 2019 (AAFP summary): a frozen limb should not be used for walking or climbing until care is reached. AHA 2024: avoid walking on frozen feet when possible.
