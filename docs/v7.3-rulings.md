# v7.3 rulings and deferred items (heat pack)

Decisions Claude made during the v7.3 build without asking Jordan, each with what it costs if wrong, in the order they
were made. Jordan can overrule any of them. The second section lists what the final review found and chose not to fix
in v7.3.

## Rulings

1. Kerosene and propane heaters (and other unvented fuel-burning heaters) get operating guidance under the strictest
   conditions any opened agency source sets, instead of a blanket "don't use them indoors". The agencies and New York
   rules that advise against them are named first, in the heaters topic's first section, "What safety bodies say first".
   Why: when asked to choose between the two, Jordan said "continue" without choosing, and this was the recommended
   option: people in a long outage may use one anyway, and the strict conditions are safer than no guidance. Cost if
   wrong: the heaters topic is rewritten as a plain "don't use them indoors", with a list of what to use instead. D39
   in docs/decisions-for-jordan.md has the agencies' positions.
2. The three content batches (heating at home; wood, fuel and the house; winter storms, cold and clothing) were to be
   written one after another, not at the same time, because they share the source record and the decision numbers.
   Cost if wrong: a longer build. (Ruling 6 later changed this for the last two batches.)
3. The build plan was not sent back to Jordan for a second approval: he said go and asked for no check-ins. Cost if
   wrong: a defect in the plan is found in review instead of before the work.
4. The wind chill tool's code and data were built in a separate copy of the project, alongside the content work, and
   merged in before the winter-storms batch, because they share no files. Cost if wrong: one merge to resolve.
5. Ruling (Task 3, wind chill): every frostbite band in `src/tooldata/windchill.json` is `0` and `title` says the tool shows wind chill only and gives no frostbite time — no opened NWS/NOAA page gives the chart's shaded areas as text (the national chart is an image; the one image link found, weather.gov/ama windchill.gif, failed to fetch; text tables carry no shading), and a guessed or formula-derived boundary could understate danger — cost if wrong: users get no "frostbite in N minutes" line even where the NWS chart shades one; the title points them to the 30-minute example and to the protocols instead.
6. The last two content batches (winter storms, cold and clothing; snow shelters and fire) were written at the same
   time in separate copies, each with its own block of decision numbers (from 50 and from 60), which removed the
   shared-file clash that made ruling 2 sequential. Cost if wrong: the source record, decisions file and topic-count
   test had to be merged by hand.
7. The boiler caution that has no source (wait for a heating contractor before relighting a drained hot-water system)
   stays in Protect an unheated house, labelled as the app's own caution, with D48. Why: it only holds a person back
   from an action, and the reviewer judged it safe. Cost if wrong: an unnecessary wait before relighting a boiler.
8. The diagrams were drawn for the approved batches while the winter-storms batch was still being fixed, and no
   diagrams were made for that batch in this pack: one of clothing layers would not be recognisable without its
