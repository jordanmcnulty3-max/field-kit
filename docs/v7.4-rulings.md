## Tool gate

**PASS (provisional: the batch A reviewer must re-open the source and confirm it, as the plan requires).**

- Source: FEMA Center for Domestic Preparedness (CDP), online course AWR-923-W "Radiological Emergency Management"
  (course folder dated May 12, 2021; listed on the CDP Online Courses page, whose logo reads "Department of Homeland
  Security Shield / FEMA Center for Domestic Preparedness"). Pages:
  - "The 7:10 Rule of Thumb": https://cdp.dhs.gov/shared/se/courses/default/AWR-923-W%2005122021%201.2-20210512144644/groups/394.html
  - "Limitations of 7:10 Rule of Thumb": https://cdp.dhs.gov/shared/se/courses/default/AWR-923-W%2005122021%201.2-20210512144644/groups/395.html
  - Catalogue: https://cdp.dhs.gov/online_course
- Exact wording (as fetched, in two pieces because of the fetch quote limit): "The 7:10 Rule of Thumb states that for every
  7-fold increase in time after detonation," / "there is a 10-fold decrease in the exposure rate." Worked example on the
  page: "let's say that 2 hours after detonation the exposure rate is 400 R/hr." / "After 14 hours, the exposure rate will
  be 1/10 as much, or 40 R/hr."
- Limits on the second page: "Like any rule of thumb, the answers obtained are only approximations."; the rule assumes the
  detonation time is known and fallout from only one detonation; "For accuracy and reliability, nothing can replace a
  direct instrument reading."
- Rows the source gives: the rule as text, and one worked pair (2 hours to 14 hours = 1/10). It prints no table. Rows that
  follow from its text, relative to 1 hour: 7 hours = 1/10; 49 hours = 1/100; 343 hours (about 2 weeks) = 1/1,000. FEMA's
  1985 Protection in the Nuclear Age prints exactly these three (7 hours; "49 hours or approximately two days"; "about two
  weeks ... one-thousandth of the level at one hour"), but it is pre-2000 and may be used only as dated text, not for the
  tool.
- Why provisional: the CDP is FEMA's training center, but the course pages themselves do not name FEMA (the catalogue logo
  does); it is responder training, not public guidance; and it gives the rule as text with one example rather than a
  table. If the reviewer reads "rows it gives" strictly as printed rows, or does not accept a CDP course as a FEMA source,
  the gate is FAIL and Tasks 2 and 3 are skipped; surv-fallout-how-long's 7:10 section then stays as text (current CDP
  wording plus the dated 1985 table) with no tool link.
- Looked for, not found (current sources with the rule as text or table): FEMA, Planning Guidance for Response to a Nuclear
  Detonation, third edition (2022), all of it (only "Fallout gives off over half of its energy in the first hour and then
  continues to decay rapidly" and other qualitative lines); FEMA, Nuclear Detonation Response Guidance: Planning for the
  First 72 Hours (2023; qualitative only, "decrease over the first 48 hours"); CDC public pages (Get Inside, Stay Inside,
  What to Do, Where To Go, Preparing) and CDC clinician pages (Immediate Actions, Preparing for Radiation Incidents); Ready.gov
  Radiation Emergencies ("Radiation levels decrease rapidly ... during the first 24 hours"); HHS REMM (search returned only
  archive copies of REMM pages; not used); EPA (search returned only snapshot archives, including an EPA-hosted copy of the
  superseded 2010 second edition of the planning guidance; not used); DHS S&T "Visualizing a Nuclear Detonation" fact sheet
  (none). The LLNL report "Key Response Planning Factors for the Aftermath of Nuclear Terrorism" is hosted by LLNL, not
  published by FEMA, and was not opened.

## Meter

No official source opened gives meter readings a member of the public can act on. Looked in: Ready.gov Radiation
Emergencies; CDC Get Inside, Stay Inside, What to Do, Where To Go, Preparing for a Radiation Emergency (no mention of
detectors at all); FEMA planning guidance, third edition (detectors for responders, and planner zone thresholds only);
FEMA 72-hour guidance ("Only emergency workers with radiation detection equipment should operate outdoors"); the CDP
7:10 pages ("nothing can replace a direct instrument reading"). The dated 1968 and 1985 handbooks say only that without
instruments you depend on local government to say when to leave. So surv-fallout-how-long's "If you have a meter" section
says a meter is the only way to know the level and that the app does not yet explain how to read one, and meter guidance
is deferred (below). The tool, if built, takes no meter input (spec 6, call 4).

## Rulings

1. Links to content that does not exist yet are left out, because tools/validate.py rejects an unknown link key ("unknown
   link"). Later tasks add them (list below). Cost if wrong: none; Task 8 tests every link.
2. tests/test_build.py's pinned topic count was raised from 38 to 42 for the four new topics, outside the files this task
   listed, because the full suite must pass before the commit. Cost if wrong: one line to revert.
3. The current sheltering period is "at least 24 hours" (Ready.gov, CDC, NYC Health), not FEMA's planning figure of 12 to
   24 hours: the longer is used, and the topic says so. Cost if wrong: none to safety.
4. Fallout arrival is given as "10 minutes or more" (Ready.gov, FEMA). FEMA's "an hour or more in many areas", its 72-hour
   guidance's "several hours" and the old handbooks' "15 to 30 minutes" are not shown, so nobody delays getting inside (D74).
   Cost if wrong: people far from a blast are not told they may have longer.
5. The dated handbooks (Office of Civil Defense 1968, FEMA 1985) are used in three places only: how long to stay when no
   word comes (D68), the basement corner most below ground (current guidance says only "basement"), and the 7:10 table rows
   (as dated text). Their camp-stove advice is named and not followed (D73). Cost if wrong: none to safety.
6. With no official word, the app plans on up to 2 weeks (the longer of the two handbooks, plus 1985's "somewhat longer in
   limited areas"), with a labelled app caution on trips (D68). Cost if wrong: people stay in longer than they need to.
7. The Army survival manual is used only for "In the woods with no building" (spec 10, call 11), from FM 3-05.70 chapter 23
   in a county-hosted printout of a mirror (lines cut at the margin; no manual date). FM 21-76 could not be read that far
   (fetch limit). The Army post-attack timetable is not used (call 11), and is described in D68. Cost if wrong: the woods
   steps rest on a mirror copy; a reviewer with the original may find a word cut off.
8. "Don't tape or cover doors, windows or vents to make the house airtight" is labelled as the app's reading of FEMA's
   "Structures do not need to be airtight to protect against fallout radiation" and "maintain enough ventilation to ensure
   adequate indoor air quality". No opened source says "don't tape" in words. Cost if wrong: one sentence goes beyond its
   source; it only holds people back from sealing a house.
9. A furnace or boiler vented to the outside that still has power is treated as the house's normal heat, not as the "stove
   or heater" of Jordan's winter rule (D76). Cost if wrong: a household runs its furnace before the cold is a danger to life.
10. FEMA's shelter-move timing (poor shelter 30 minutes, better one an hour or two, adequate shelter within 15 minutes) is
    included without its protection factor numbers, with a note that FEMA does not say which buildings count as poor (D72).
    Cost if wrong: readers may misjudge which shelter is "poor".
11. New York is represented by New York City's pages (NYC Health and the city's Response Guide), named as city guidance:
    searches found no New York State Department of Health or DHSES page on a nuclear detonation. Cost if wrong: a state
    page, if one exists, is not cited.
12. The hub links Severe bleeding, Burns and Shock for injuries, with Ready.gov's "listen for instructions on how and where to
    get medical attention"; Ready.gov's "call 9-1-1" line is left out (no dial control). Cost if wrong: none.

13. The hub's title is "Fallout warning: what to do first", not the plan's "what to do now": with "now" in the title, a
    search for "no" listed the hub before the CPR protocol (tests/test_search.py pins "no" to CPR, adult). The spec lets the
    builder adjust title wording. Cost if wrong: wording only.
14. tests/test_reports.py::test_release_gate_no_longer_blocks_on_report_wording runs a --release build of the real tree, so
    it fails while any topic is draft. The four topics stay draft in this dispatch as the plan requires; the test passes
    once draft is removed in the fix round. It was not edited. Cost if wrong: one known failure until the fix round.
